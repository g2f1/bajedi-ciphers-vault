Before introducing the ciphers that are the focus of this study, it is useful to first present the general classification of cryptographic algorithms. The figure below illustrates a commonly used classification of cryptographic algorithms, separating them into **classical** and **modern** ciphers ([source](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Classification-of-cryptographic-algorithms_fig4_330440535)).

![classification](../assets/classifcation.png)

In this work, we focus on **classical ciphers**. As shown in the figure, they are divided into two main categories: **substitution ciphers** and **transposition ciphers**.

A **substitution cipher** replaces each symbol of the plaintext with another symbol from the ciphertext alphabet according to a predefined substitution rule while preserving the order of the symbols. In contrast, a **transposition cipher** keeps the original symbols unchanged but rearranges their positions according to a predefined permutation.
